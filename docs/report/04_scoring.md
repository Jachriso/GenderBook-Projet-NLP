# 5. Semantic scoring and recommendation (P4)

The engine turns the questionnaire answers into a **semantic profile**, then scores each book with an explicit and **fully traceable** formula.

**User profile.** All answers become text encoded by SBERT (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, normalised vectors). The global query $q_{global}$ is the average of the embeddings of the three free-text answers. The average, preferred to concatenation, gives each question the same weight and avoids truncation at 256 tokens. If the favourite author is found in the catalogue, the centroid of their books is mixed in (0.7 / 0.3). For each block $b$ (Genre, Themes, Style, Atmosphere), we build the set $P_b(u)$ of preference sentences: the descriptive sentences of the ticked labels, the related free text, and above all the **Likerts converted into sentences**. For example, a complexity of 5 becomes "*a complex, dense, literary and demanding narrative*". A numeric answer is thus compared with the books through its meaning. The neutral level (3) produces no sentence.

**Formula.** With $e_j$ the embedding of book $j$ and $L_b(j)$ its labels assigned by P2:

$$S_{desc}(j) = \cos(q_{global}, e_j) \qquad S_b(j) = \frac{1}{|P_b|}\sum_{p \in P_b(u)} \max_{l \in L_b(j) \cup \{e_j\}} \cos(p, l)$$

$$\tilde s = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{s - p_5}{p_{95} - p_5}, 0, 1\right) \qquad \text{Coverage}(j) = \frac{\sum_b W_b\, \tilde S_b(j)}{\sum_b W_b}$$

$$\text{Final}(j) = \frac{\alpha\, \tilde S_{desc}(j) + \beta\, \text{Coverage}(j) + \lambda\, \text{Pop}(j)}{\alpha + \beta + \lambda} - \mu \max\big(0, \cos(q_{avoid}, e_j) - \tau\big)$$

with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\beta = 0.4$, $\lambda \in [0 ; 0.2]$, $\mu = 0.5$ and $\tau = 0.3$ (constants in `src/config.py`).

**Justification.** $\tilde S_{desc}$ compares two free texts (the request and the blurb): it is the richest semantic signal, hence the highest weight. The **coverage**, required by the assignment, checks that the book answers *each* requested dimension. Its weights $W_b$ are the importance Likerts: the user decides. The "max then mean" rule follows the lecturer's example: each preference is satisfied by the book's best label, and the book must answer all of them. Adding the blurb $e_j$ to the max makes the score robust when a label is missing. Popularity remains a small bonus set by a Likert. Dividing by $\alpha + \beta + \lambda$ guarantees $\text{Final} \in [0,1]$: the *Excellent* (≥ 0.75), *Good* (≥ 0.55) and *Partial* thresholds keep the same meaning for everyone. The "to avoid" text is encoded **without its negations**, because SBERT handles negation poorly ("*no romance*" resembles "*romance*").

**Calibration.** SBERT cosines are compressed, hence the percentile calibration. We compute $p_5$ and $p_{95}$ **over the 300 candidates retrieved for 8 reference personas**, and not over the whole catalogue. Indeed, the retrieval keeps the top 2.5%: all candidates exceed the catalogue's p95, and $\tilde S_{desc}$ would be 1 for all of them. The calibration is then frozen, so it is comparable across users.

**Pipeline and explanations.** `recommend(answers)` applies the hard filters (audience, format, excluded author, and fiction only, enabled automatically when only fiction genres are ticked). These filters are relaxed step by step if no book passes. Then come a retrieval of the 300 closest books, a vectorised re-ranking (numpy) and a diversification (one book per author). Each recommendation contains its score, its label (computed on the rounded score shown), the score breakdown (the sum of the contributions gives the final score), the shared labels, the sentence of the blurb closest to the request and a **template-generated explanation**. The explanation only cites labels requested by the user, never a label that contradicts an answer (no "fast-paced" for a slow pace).

**Results and limitations.** On 12,000 books with MiniLM, genre precision@3 reaches 0.92 on our 8 personas, against 0.75 with the global similarity alone. Increasing the theme weight raises, weakly, the Themes score of the top 10 (0.357 → 0.373). The tests revealed an important limitation: **subject proximity is not relevance**. For the *dark academia* persona, the word "secrets" pulled up *My Secret: A PostSecret Book*, a non-fiction collection that P2 had wrongly labelled *Thriller & Suspense* and that P1 classified as fiction. We fixed it: when only fiction genres are ticked, non-fiction and collections are filtered out automatically. The risk remains if the user ticks both fiction and non-fiction. With $\lambda \le 0.2$ and $\tau = 0.3$, the popularity bonus and the "to avoid" penalty barely change the top 10: their values will be tuned by P6's ablation. Finally, the calibration relies on 8 personas, and the lack of series data prevents the "standalone" filter.
